## Required contribution with scientific qualifications

Fossil Hunt is required baseline curriculum despite its `extension_` filename. Cross-site correlation and relative-age ordering strengthen the ESS1-4 performance pathway. Required status does not validate every scientific shortcut, establish independent reasoning, or change the frozen C classification.

## Reviewed findings

| Finding | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Broad fossil-group labels are treated as diagnostic index markers | The name alone does not support the claimed narrow correlation interval |
| “Appears once in the displayed site” substitutes for short geologic duration | A display constraint is not evidence of an organism's short temporal range |
| Crinoid treatment lacks sufficient taxonomic specificity | Broad Crinoidea has a long history including living forms; particular taxa can be useful without making the whole group a short-duration marker |
| Same-marker feedback overstates same-age inference | Correlation requires scientifically sufficient range/context evidence |
| Challenge A1/A2 age/position contradiction and “most recent” final-check problem | Ordinary indigenous-fossil ordering and the displayed ranges are internally inconsistent |
| Rugose-coral start age error; ammonite/graptolite range-scope questions | Age labels and taxonomic scope need coordinated future correction |
| Overgeneralized rejection of fragments/plant impressions | Preservation form alone does not universally determine correlation utility |
| Hints and feedback teach the one-layer shortcut | Scaffolds can reinforce invalid reasoning rather than merely reduce difficulty |
| Reset state can bypass a fresh age check | Completion state may carry across a reset |
| Submission omits full configuration/reasoning chain | Summary success does not establish how the correlation was reached |

These are the human-reviewed scientific/task findings; the activity was not corrected in this session. Detailed scientific checks and source provenance are in Investigation 3, sections 5–18 and 24–27, identified in the [source register](source-register.md).

## Human decisions for later correction

Grade 6 specificity need not become a taxonomy lesson. Correlation must nevertheless rest on scientifically sufficient evidence. One diagnostic marker can be enough; assemblages are not universally required. Hints may scaffold but must not teach false shortcuts. Guided completion and independent evidence must remain distinguishable.

The lesson's legitimate relative-age/correlation opportunity should be retained while scientific and evidence qualifications remain visible. Do not infer that all fossil-based learning is invalid or that the activity must be removed.

## Retention and external-review implications

The retained summary does not preserve the full arrangement, age-order attempts, scaffold use, and reasoning. A score/completion result is narrower than an independently justified correlation. Scientific accuracy, evidence retention, and interpretation are separate future work areas.

ESS1-4 remains a future classification-reconsideration candidate under explicit human review. This record makes no change to C and assigns no external-review score.

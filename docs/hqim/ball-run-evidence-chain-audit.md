# Ball Run Evidence-Chain Audit — Investigation 2

> **Status:** Human-reviewed Astra read-only audit consolidated 2026-09-27. Sources: Investigation 2; HQIM-5; owner trial policies and summer-program clarification; [source register](source-register.md). Human policy belongs to the project owner. This record authorizes no implementation. Source behavior is not proof of deployed behavior or teacher receipt.

## Provenance and curriculum status

**Ball Run originated as an experimental Engineering Challenge used in a summer program with a small number of students.** It is now required baseline Grade 6 curriculum alongside Build-a-Boat.

Its multi-day instructional structure provides useful evidence about testing, iteration, reasoning, evidence collection, and Engineering Challenge design. Current local state, data retention, PDF/email delivery, and evidence-return mechanisms are **prototype behavior, not the intended final LyfeLabz architecture**. The owner plans to revisit persistence, evidence return/push-back, and cumulative challenge records. The audit informs that later work without treating the experiment as a finished product.

## Human-ratified evidence policy

1. Success means the ball travels from designated entrance to exit unaided without violating constraints.
2. Duration is the primary performance measure for successful trials.
3. Failed/stalled trials remain in the engineering record.
4. Reliability establishes viability; duration determines optimization among viable designs.
5. Every recorded trial remains part of the record.
6. Physical construction/testing may be team work.
7. Shared quantitative measurements may be team evidence.
8. Predictions, evidence interpretation, failure diagnosis, redesign reasoning, comparisons, and final reflection should remain individually attributable.
9. Trade-off reasoning is required.

These are intended instructional policies. Current prototype completion does not fully enforce or preserve them.

## Reviewed daily evidence chain

| Stage | Instructional opportunity | Prototype qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Criteria/constraints, initial prediction and planning | Criteria largely supplied; checklist gate and overview sketch claims do not establish all actions |
| Day 2 | Physical first build and repeated trials | Time/distance optional; written fields do not prove actual construction |
| Day 3 | Prior evidence → one declared change → retest/comparison | Declaration of one change does not enforce physical control; quantitative comparison not required |
| Day 4 | Select strongest version, adjust, run repeated tests, judge reliability | “Met goal” can lack duration; denominator can include incomplete rows; applied trade-off evidence not required |
| Day 5 | Explain changes and final design | Does not import all prior records or verify prior-day completion; report is Day 5 rather than cumulative challenge history |

The audit confirmed that Days 2–4 could complete with time and distance blank. Duration-based optimization therefore was not established by completion alone.

## Prototype retention and delivery findings

- Local browser work and daily PDFs preserve meaningful evidence, but a cumulative authenticated Workspace record was not established.
- The report backend discards accompanying structured answers; allowable trial text can be shortened in the PDF.
- Editing an already confirmed record did not invalidate the confirmed state in synthetic audit checks across all five days. Current visible/downloaded work could differ from emailed evidence.
- Mutable trials and no required full failure record allow selective reporting; evidence carry-forward is instructional rather than automatic.
- Editable names, shared local keys, grade-only class fields, and a fixed teacher-email route limit authenticated assignment/team/individual attribution.
- PDF generation does not prove saving; email API success does not prove inbox receipt or teacher review.

These findings describe the experimental mechanism, not a requirement to preserve it or a judgment against a completed production architecture.

## Future interpretation

Evaluate reliability and duration separately, retain failed trials, and credit sound reasoning even when a design performs poorly. Preserve the engineering identity of plans and revisions. The conceptual direction is documented in [engineering principles](engineering-challenge-principles.md), without schemas or implementation contracts.

## Official-Source Findings Integrated in Phase 2C

These findings were verified in Phase 2B on 2026-09-19 and integrated here in
Phase 2C. There is no established mandatory EQuIP-to-EdReports-to-CURATE
approval sequence. Program review and lesson/unit review are separate targets.

**EdReports comprehensive-program review.** This is independent external review.
The current framework is
[EdReports Science 6-8 Review Criteria and Evidence Guide v2.0 (July 2025)](https://edreports.org/process/review-tools/science).
Its gateways are 1: Designed for NGSS; 2: Coherence and Scope; and 3: Teacher
and Student Supports. Gateway 1 Meets or Partial permits Gateway 2; Gateway 3
requires Meets in both Gateways 1 and 2, including Gateway 2's non-negotiable
indicators. The current criteria PDF and evidence guide conflict on Criterion
1.2 and 3.2 scoring ranges. No range is silently adopted here; clarification
is needed before future scoring work. [Review selection is discretionary](https://edreports.org/about/faqs);
a request does not guarantee acceptance. This report assigns no EdReports score.

**CURATE Massachusetts program review.** DESE's
[CURATE process](https://www.doe.mass.edu/instruction/curate/process.html) is active
for science/STE and accepts publisher submissions at no review fee. A qualifying
prior independent alignment review is required. EdReports is an accepted route;
DESE may approve another independent review under the
[COMMBUYS solicitation, Amendment 6](https://www.commbuys.com/bso/external/bidDetail.sda?docId=BD-23-1026-DOE02-DOE01-77790&external=true&parentUrl=close).
CURATE is not an automatic next stage after EdReports, and an EQuIP unit report
is not established as a qualifying whole-program review.

The [current STE rubric](https://www.doe.mass.edu/instruction/curate/ste-rubric.docx)
has two domains. Standards Alignment includes Scope and Progression and Approach
to Instruction. Classroom Application includes Accessibility for Students,
Usability for Teachers, and Impact on Learning. Impact on Learning receives
narrative treatment without a rating. These criteria are separate from
EdReports gateways and from this report's fourteen internal topics.

**EQuIP lesson/unit review.** The [science rubric v3.1](https://www.nextgenscience.org/resources/equip-rubric-science)
has three categories: NGSS 3D Design; NGSS Instructional Supports; Monitoring
NGSS Student Progress. It supports internal development and external
lesson/unit evaluation, not comprehensive-program gateway scoring. It is not
established as a mandatory EdReports or CURATE prerequisite.
[NextGenScience's external service](https://ngs.wested.org/reviews-of-science-and-engineering-instructional-materials-and-assessment/)
offers paid reviews; top-rated full unit reviews may qualify for an NGSS Design
Badge. The [free Peer Review Panel intake is paused](https://www.nextgenscience.org/peer-review-panel/nextgenscience-peer-review-panel).
A successful unit review establishes neither whole-program quality nor automatic
entry to EdReports/CURATE. No review acceptance or outreach has occurred here.

**Massachusetts high-quality terminology and procurement.** DESE
[operationally identifies qualifying materials as high quality](https://www.doe.mass.edu/instruction/curate/default.html)
using specified external-review outcomes. This is not a universal certification
or a mandatory science adoption decision. Qualifying CURATE ratings can establish
[Departmental Master Agreement eligibility](https://www.doe.mass.edu/instruction/curate/master-agreement.html);
contracting and local district purchasing remain separate. Ratings do not
guarantee purchases, reimbursement, state purchasing, or grant funding. Grant
conditions are program-specific; Phase 2B did not establish an automatic science
funding route.

**Unresolved admission scope.** Grade-span eligibility for LyfeLabz's Grade
6-only and Grades 6-7 configurations remains to be confirmed directly with
EdReports and DESE CURATE. Neither narrower-span admission nor complete 6-8
coverage as a universal admission requirement was established. Grade-band
scoring scope is not an admission rule. Do not decide to build Grade 8 solely
on that assumption. A lesson/unit review is a separate possibility without a
complete Grade 8 program.

Other Phase 2B questions remain unresolved: the two EdReports scoring conflicts,
its inconsistent purchase/temporary-access wording, CURATE's descriptions of
review depth, DESE dashboard wording versus the newer high-quality definition,
and current queue/access arrangements. This document does not settle them or
promise a submission date. Direct clarification and a dedicated pathway-specific
readiness analysis belong to later work.

## 1. Three-Dimensional Learning

**Internal assessment: substantial evidence, with interpretation corrections.**

Amplitude Challenge requires trial data and interpretation. Eclipse activities
let students operate alignment models. Floatia and ball-run require physical
artifacts, testing, and revision. These are substantive opportunities to combine
content, practices, and reasoning concepts.

The map must distinguish using supplied models from developing models, selected
explanations from constructed explanations, and authored feedback from student
reasoning. Layer Detective displays an authored geologic story after a correct
ordering; that story is not written by the student. Earth's Place supplies a
nested zoom model rather than an independent visual-model construction task.
Repeated examples or topical association alone do not establish a CCC.

Conducting Experiments explicitly labels SEP 1/3/4 and Continental Drift labels
SEP-4. The former claim of no lesson SEP labels is false. Supporting pages use
mixed notation. Terminology consistency is a later review item, not a reason to
mechanically add or downgrade labels. Lexical prediction/reveal counts are not
counts of instructional interactions.

## 2. Performance-Expectation Alignment

**Internal assessment: broad instruction, incomplete performance verification.**

The Grade 6 map separates instructional coverage from student performance and
assessment evidence. Phase 1 flagged ESS1-5(MA), LS4-1, LS4-2, ETS1-6(MA), and
ETS2-3(MA). Phase 2B confirmed that ESS1-5(MA) requires use of graphical displays,
not independent construction; that historical qualification must be interpreted
accordingly. PS1-6 requires an implemented investigation, not just a badge
correction. PS1-8(MA) also explicitly requires a conducted experiment; existing
classification/method-selection evidence does not establish that performance.
The map contains eight `(MA)` codes, five in engineering. Historical audit totals
and all coverage classifications remain unchanged pending curriculum review.

Biological Evolution includes anatomical CER but also substantial
natural-selection mechanism content and quiz emphasis. Grade-boundary
compliance cannot be inferred from changed badges. Some lessons lack PE badges
by design, and supporting activities can provide evidence without badges.

Layer Detective, Amplitude Challenge, and Cell Energy's original unprefixed
badge defects are resolved. Gravity Wells (`MS-ESS1-2`) and Floatlandia Fracture
(`ESS1-4`, `ESS2-2`) still need curriculum review of codes and descriptions.
This report does not decide replacement standards.

## 3. Phenomena and Design Problems

**Internal assessment: strong examples; uniform implementation not established.**

Waves, eclipses, matter puzzles, and engineering challenges provide inspectable
entries into reasoning. Interaction depth varies. The gap-unit specs describe
future phenomena and cannot support ratings of current instruction. Vocabulary

## 4. Sense-Making

**Internal assessment: real opportunities, with response-type qualifications.**

Students make predictions, interpret models, apply ideas, and write synthesis.
All 50 root lessons contain a textarea. Receiving an explanation or choosing
one can support learning, but does not demonstrate independent explanation
construction. The new distinction should govern citations, not a universal
claim that sense-making is either absent or fully established.

## 6. Integrated Assessment

**Internal assessment: selected-response assessment is established; performance
evaluation and capture are uneven.**

The 49 authenticated lesson payloads contain 495 `singleChoice` items: Body
Systems has 15, and the other 48 configured lessons have ten each. Ragebaiting
is outside this 49-lesson build/payload population. Writing and physical
artifacts are additional instructional evidence, not automatically scored PE
attainment.

For the two inspected LS writing tasks, submission requires text and reveals a
model response, but the authenticated `lessonQuiz.finalize` calls send only
selected answers. Legacy public submission code includes a `thinking` field.
Production receipt was not tested. Do not generalize task existence into an
assurance that the platform preserves every student artifact.

Ball-run already has trial tables, controlled revisions, comparisons, and
reports. Floatia requires a scaled drawing, tests, and defense. Review their
rubrics and individual evidence rather than assuming data capture is absent.

Planned gap-unit graph/model/history/written submissions are not currently
implemented. `platform/functions/src/shared/types/assessment.ts` defines only
`singleChoice`. Such artifact submission needs an explicit architecture decision
before the specs can be treated as runtime-compatible build instructions.

## 7. Scaffolding and Accessibility

**Internal assessment: baseline features exist; conformance and instructional

All 50 root lessons contain `a11y-canonical`, `mobile-canonical`,

Their presence supports a baseline implementation claim, not certification of
keyboard, screen-reader, contrast, or other accessibility outcomes. Scaffolding
and multimodal supporting activities exist, but consistent multilingual and
learner-specific guidance is not established across the catalog.

## 8. Teacher Support

**Internal assessment: distributed and uneven, not absent.**

Educator Mode/educator-note markup exists in all 50 root lessons. Forty-nine

root-only. Floatia's Teacher Playbook contains preparation, materials per team,

No consolidated curriculum teacher guide was located. The root
`EDUCATOR_MODE_GUIDE.md` describes implementation of the overlay, not a
consolidated curriculum guide. A future guide should extract canonical educator
content and reference separately maintained unit guidance, with source hashes
and drift checks, rather than create a second editable copy of lesson notes.

## 13. Assessment Integrity

**Internal assessment: narrow implementation strengths are supported.**

Authenticated selected-response attempts are finalized server-side in
`platform/functions/src/assessments/assessment-attempts-finalize.ts`.
`platform/firebase/firestore.rules` denies client create/update/delete on
attempts. These controls support the application's record-integrity design;
they do not establish that privileged infrastructure can never change a record.

The inspected constructed-response limitation in Section 6 also prevents a
claim that every reviewed piece of student work is preserved in an attempt.
No security audit or production test was performed. Claims of national-exemplar
quality or superiority to other curricula are removed as unsupported comparisons.

## 14. Equity and Access

**Internal assessment: useful baseline, unresolved delivery and access evidence.**

Public lesson outputs provide an anonymous instructional surface; authenticated
assignment delivery is a different mode. Source accessibility features,
scaffolding, and some extensions exist. Their existence is not an accessibility
certification, proof of universal access, or evidence of active multilingual
and differentiated production delivery. Final differentiation/access statements
require the owning workstream and targeted verification.

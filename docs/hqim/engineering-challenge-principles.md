# Engineering Challenges: Curriculum and Evidence Principles

> **Status:** Internal record consolidated 2026-09-27 from human-reviewed Astra read-only audits and project-owner decisions. Sources: owner Build-a-Boat account and policies; HQIM-3A/4A/5; Investigation 2; [source register](source-register.md). No implementation, classification change, or production certification is authorized.

## Architectural direction

**Assignment → Engineering Challenge → Stages → Team + Individual Evidence → Iterations → Final Evidence**

This is an emerging curriculum/product concept, not a fully implemented platform capability. It is not a schema or implementation contract. Both Ball Run and Build-a-Boat are required baseline Grade 6 challenges; their relative sequence is not settled.

Physical construction can remain physical. Team measurements can be shared. Individual reasoning should remain attributable. Failed trials are legitimate evidence, iteration history matters, and engineering outcome does not equal engineering reasoning quality. The highest-performing design does not automatically represent the strongest engineering thinking.

## Authentic classroom Build-a-Boat

The owner's classroom account is authoritative for curriculum analysis. Teams work with aluminum foil, wax paper, modeling clay, and balsa wood to hold the most pennies within material prices and a budget. They prepare scaled drawings before first store access and explain their design rationale, then construct, test, record quantitative penny results, analyze failure, redesign, and build again. Individual reflections and daily logs accompany team construction/testing.

HQIM-5 records three classroom build cycles with intervening individual reflections and redesign reasoning. This is human-supplied classroom context; the audit did not directly observe enactment or inspect a complete set of student artifacts.

## Ratified round policy

- Each round receives a fresh **1,000-unit budget**.
- Unused currency does not carry forward.
- Previously purchased materials are not reused.
- Materials are discarded after each build because they may be damaged or altered.
- Team construction/testing can coexist with individually attributable reasoning.

These settled policies supersede any older inference that materials or balances carry over.

## Current digital Floatia representation

The [Floatia page](../../challenge_welcome-to-floatia.html) contains material choices, scaled-drawing guidance, paper/physical testing, defense/redesign, and a Teacher Playbook. It is useful repository evidence but is not an exact substitute for the authentic classroom protocol.

Its digital quiz/local responses do not fully represent classroom drawings, purchase decisions, penny tests, logs, individual reflections, or repeated-build history. Paper evidence is legitimate; a platform representation gap must not erase the physical curriculum. Tool-use and individual participation need appropriate observation/evaluation, not automatic inference from the team boat.

Local-state/PDF/email mechanisms are not a final product commitment. Planned evidence persistence, student return/push-back, and cumulative records remain future owner-directed work.

## Teacher interpretation

Distinguish criteria compliance, reliability, quantitative performance, fair comparison, material/cost trade-offs, failure diagnosis, scale/communication quality, and the justification for revision. Shared outcomes and individual reasoning answer different questions. Direct observation can establish practical participation without recording every action digitally.

No rubric, schema, new activity, final architecture, or implementation schedule is adopted by this document.

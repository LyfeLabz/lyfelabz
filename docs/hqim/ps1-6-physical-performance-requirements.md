> **Status:** Internal record consolidated 2026-09-27 from human-reviewed Astra read-only audits and project-owner decisions. ## Human-ratified requirement

The future implementation must support meaningful student planning and participation in authentic physical experimentation, quantitative temperature evidence, and reasoning about thermal-energy release and absorption. Both thermal directions are needed for full scope. Existing explanations, quizzes, and experimental preparation do not establish that performance. Frozen classification: **D**, physical classroom implementation gap.

The governing wording is Massachusetts PS1-6, not a substituted national NGSS device-design task. The state clarification includes dissolution examples, so a blanket exclusion of dissolution would be inappropriate. This statement does not select chemicals or a process. [Massachusetts framework, Grade 6 physical science](https://www.doe.mass.edu/frameworks/scitech/2016-04.pdf#page=60).

## Minimum defensible evidence chain

**Purpose → attributable student planning → actual qualifying physical process → student/team measurements and observations → retained results → individual thermal interpretation → supported conclusion.**

| Element | Requirement/qualification |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | May be teacher supplied |
| Planning | Students make meaningful experimental decisions within safety/material constraints; collaborative planning can count when contributions are attributable |
| Conducting | Meaningful physical participation; following a wholly supplied procedure alone does not establish the planning component |
| Measurement | Quantitative temperature evidence relevant to change and direction; actual results, not substituted ideal values |
| Scope | Both release and absorption represented; a warming-only or cooling-only experience is partial evidence |
| Interpretation | Each student connects measurements and context to supported thermal-energy transfer |
| Retention/visibility | Shared team data, individual reasoning, paper/digital records, and teacher observation can be legitimate |

## Temperature and energy

Temperature is evidence used to reason about thermal-energy transfer, not an interchangeable name for energy or a direct measurement of energy quantity. Interpretation needs a stated system/surroundings context and attention to what was actually measured. The future task must not teach that a temperature reading alone proves all chemical or energetic claims.

## Team evidence and productive uncertainty

Shared measurements may support individual analysis. Each student's planning, physical participation, and reasoning must remain attributable through an appropriate combination of artifacts and teacher observation. Unexpected, noisy, or ambiguous results are real evidence; conclusions should acknowledge limitations rather than replace observations with expected outcomes.

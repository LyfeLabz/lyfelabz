# LyfeLabz Concept Families

The seven conceptual families that organize the Grade 6-7 curriculum as one
two-year arc, following the framework's progression from Structure and
Function (Grade 6) to Systems and Cycles (Grade 7). Families are narratives,
not tracks: a family may be weighted toward one grade, and its units connect
through the Connections layer without implying a required progression. Unit
names and numbering below match `docs/hqim/scope-and-sequence.md`.

## 1. Living Systems

Living things are built from interacting structures, from organelles inside a
cell to organisms inside an ecosystem, and the same structure-and-function
reasoning explains every scale.

- **Grade 6 units:** Unit 1 (What Is Life? Cells and Their Structures),
  Unit 2 (Body Systems)
- **Grade 7 units:** Unit 3 (Ecosystems: Structure and Flow), Unit 4
  (Ecosystem Stability and Human Impacts), Unit 5 planned (Protecting
  Ecosystems: A Design Evaluation; shared with Engineered Systems)

## 2. Life Over Time

The fossil record and anatomical comparison provide evidence that life forms
change, and the structures and behaviors of living things exist because they
helped ancestors survive and reproduce.

- **Grade 6 units:** Unit 3 (Evidence of Change: Fossils and Evolution)
- **Grade 7 units:** the reproductive-success strand of Unit 3 (Ecosystems:
  Structure and Flow); no standalone Grade 7 unit

## 3. Dynamic Earth

Earth's surface records its own history in rock and keeps changing through
plate processes, from evidence students can read (layers, fossils, continental
fit) to mechanisms and hazards they can forecast.

- **Grade 6 units:** Unit 4 (Earth's Story in Rock)
- **Grade 7 units:** Unit 1 (Earth's Dynamic Surface)

## 4. Sun, Gravity, and Earth's Cycles

The Sun's energy and gravity are the two engines that drive Earth's
large-scale patterns, from lunar phases and eclipses to the water cycle.

- **Grade 6 units:** Unit 5 (Earth in Space)
- **Grade 7 units:** Unit 2 (Water Systems)

## 5. Matter and Its Particles

All matter is made of particles, and one particulate model explains density,
observable properties, mixtures, and the energy changes of chemical reactions.

- **Grade 6 units:** Unit 6 (Matter and Its Particles)
- **Grade 7 units:** no standalone unit; the particle model is the named
  foundation for the thermal-energy strand of Unit 6 (Energy Systems)

## 6. Energy, Waves, and Fields

Energy moves through matter and space as waves, transfers between objects and
forms, and acts across distance through fields, and each of those transfers
can be modeled, measured, and graphed.

- **Grade 6 units:** Unit 7 (Waves and Signals)
- **Grade 7 units:** Unit 6 (Energy Systems), Unit 7 planned (Energy of
  Motion), Unit 8 planned (Thermal Engineering Design; shared with Engineered
  Systems)

## 7. Engineered Systems

People design solutions by defining problems, choosing materials, building and
testing prototypes, and modeling whole systems, applying science rather than
merely learning it.

- **Grade 6 units:** Unit 8 (Engineering Design), Unit 9 (Build It: The
  Floatia Capstone)
- **Grade 7 units:** Unit 9 (Engineering Systems), plus shared ownership of
  Unit 5 planned (Protecting Ecosystems) and Unit 8 planned (Thermal
  Engineering Design)

## Cross-family notes

- Fossils belong to two families on purpose: Grade 6 Unit 3 treats them as
  biological evidence (Life Over Time) before Unit 4 treats them as a dating
  tool (Dynamic Earth).
- The Waves and Signals unit (family 6) hands off directly to the
  communication-systems half of Grade 7 Unit 9 (family 7); this is the
  digital-signals bridge documented in the vertical articulation table.
- Two planned Grade 7 units are deliberately dual-family (5 and 8): each
  applies an engineering method to another family's content, which is the
  program's learn-then-apply pattern rather than a classification ambiguity.

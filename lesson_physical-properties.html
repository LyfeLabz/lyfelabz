Physical Properties of Matter

A 100,000-ton steel ship floats across the ocean, but a steel coin dropped overboard sinks instantly. Same material, opposite results. Why?

Key Vocabulary

Words You'll Meet

Select a word to explore its meaning.

Physical Property

A characteristic of a material that you can observe or measure without changing what the material is.

Color, hardness, density, and magnetism are all physical properties.

Density

How much matter is packed into a space.

Objects that are denser than water sink. Objects that are less dense than water float.

Volume

The amount of space an object takes up.

For a box shape, volume = length × width × height, in cubic centimeters (cm³).

Water Displacement

The water pushed out of the way when an object goes into it.

The water pushed aside takes up the same space as the part of the object that is underwater.

Buoyancy

The upward push that water (or another fluid) gives an object placed in it.

An object floats when this upward push is strong enough to balance its weight.

Hardness

How well a material resists being scratched or dented.

Diamond is hard enough to scratch glass. Chalk is soft enough to scratch with a fingernail.

Conductivity

How easily heat or electric current moves through a material.

Metals conduct heat and electricity well. Plastic and wood do not.

Magnetism

Whether a material is attracted to a magnet.

Iron and steel are attracted to magnets. Aluminum, copper, plastic, and wood are not.

What Is Buoyancy?

Water pushes up on anything placed in it. That upward push is called buoyancy.

Predict: What happens when you add cargo?

Takeaway: A floating ship pushes aside water that weighs the same as the ship. When the ship becomes too heavy, it sinks.

Cargo Challenge · Check your answer
The empty ship has a mass of 200 g. What is the maximum cargo mass it can carry without sinking?

Why does it float?

A ship floats because of buoyancy, the upward force of water.

  • Displacement: A ship pushes water out of the way as it sinks lower.
  • Balance: A floating ship displaces water that weighs the same as the ship.
  • Sinking: If the ship becomes too heavy to displace enough water, it sinks.

The ship's wide, hollow hull helps it displace enough water to float.

📚 Instructional Design

Physical Properties of Matter

Scientists describe and identify materials by their properties. Here is what makes a property physical.

Key idea: Physical Property

A physical property is a characteristic you can observe or measure without changing what the material is. Bending a paperclip doesn't turn it into a new metal. Melted ice is still water.

A chemical property is different: you can only observe it when a material changes into new substances, like wood burning to ash or iron rusting.

Explain · Wrap-Up

Back to the Harbor

You started with a floating steel giant, a sinking coin, and ice floating on water. Now you can explain all three.

The Answer
Sinking and floating depend on density, not weight.
The ship's hull is full of air, so the whole ship is less dense than water. The coin is solid steel, packed tight and denser than water. Ice particles lock into a spread-out pattern, so ice is less dense than liquid water.
The Tools
Three ideas work together to explain it.
Density · sink or float? Volume · how much space? Buoyancy · how big a push?
The Bigger Picture
Density is one of many physical properties.
Magnetism, conductivity, hardness, melting point: you can observe or measure each one without changing the material. Together they identify a material, like your mystery cube, and help engineers choose the right one.
Essential question: How can we predict what matter will do and identify what a material is? If you can answer with density, volume, buoyancy, and physical properties, you own this lesson.
